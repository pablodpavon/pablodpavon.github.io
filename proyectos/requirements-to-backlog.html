Case study · synthetic data Updated: October 2026

AI drafts the backlog. Every story shows who asked for it.

From a requirements workshop transcript to epics, user stories with acceptance criteria, open questions and contradictions. Every story quotes the sentence it comes from, and a code check removes it from the backlog if that sentence is not in the transcript.

Context

Turning a two-hour workshop into a backlog takes hours. A language model writes a good first draft in two minutes, with a known risk: a story that sounds right and that nobody in the room asked for.

An invented requirement costs more than a missing one, because it gets estimated, built and tested before anyone asks who wanted it.

How it works

The transcriptOne numbered turn per speaker. Three synthetic workshops: last-mile delivery, supplier onboarding in retail and class bookings at a gym chain, with contradictions and gaps on purpose.
The model draftsOne call per workshop with fixed-schema JSON output: epics, stories with Given/When/Then, open questions, assumptions and contradictions. Every item carries its quote and turn.
Code checksThat the quote is verbatim in that turn, unique IDs, no duplicates, testable criteria, and which workshop turns produced nothing.
OutputBacklog in Markdown, a CSV ready to import into Jira or Azure DevOps, a traceability matrix and a page to review each story next to its sentence.

What the check catches

In the real run the model invented none of its 200 quotes. To show what happens when it does, a copy of the real draft carries five typical mistakes planted on purpose. This is the output:

r2b check
$ python -m r2b check
last-mile-delivery: 20 drafted -> 20 in backlog (20 ready, 0 needs rework); blocked 0 ...
last-mile-delivery (planted errors): 22 drafted -> 19 in backlog (18 ready, 1 needs rework); blocked 2 unsupported, 1 duplicate ...
  unsupported   US-04  QUOTE_NOT_FOUND: "the customer must be notified within 15 minutes of a failed attempt" (claimed T05)
  needs_rework  US-19  UNTESTABLE: AC1: 'then' uses the untestable word "fast"
  unsupported   US-21  QUOTE_NOT_FOUND: "customers keep asking to see the driver live on a map" (claimed T36)
  duplicate     US-22  DUPLICATE_OF: US-05 (similarity 1.00, same source turn T18)

US-04 is the case that matters. The requirement exists: it was said in turn T05. The draft quotes a tidied-up version of that sentence, and the check blocks it anyway, because an “improved” quote cannot be told apart from an invented one without going back to the recording. With the real sentence, the story passes.

The check also listed 13 stakeholder turns that produced no item. Most are context; some are a requirement without a trace, and that list is what the analyst reads.

Limit, stated plainly: the check proves that every story comes from something someone said. Whether the story is a good one, whether the priority is right and how a contradiction is resolved is still the analyst's call.

Deliverables

  • Python pipeline: one model call per workshop, with a cost estimate and a limit before calling.
  • Deterministic check of quotes, IDs, duplicates, acceptance criteria and coverage.
  • CSV for Jira or Azure DevOps import and a traceability matrix.
  • Review page: every story next to the sentence it comes from.
  • 38 offline tests with the model simulated.
